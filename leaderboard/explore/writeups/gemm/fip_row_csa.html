fip_sign with a carry-select accumulator adder

The same player's fip_sign with two more changes, in two modules: the weight-correction unit's signed 16 x 16 products are written as unsigned cores and sign rows, as fip_sign's cell product is, and the row's 48-bit accumulator add is split into six 8-bit carry-select slices. The row stays the slowest module, but its period falls from 1,809 to 1,735 ps, and the score goes from 0.685 to 0.657 × par, the board's best when it was entered.

What it was built on

The design file is fip_sign's (tasks/gemm/reference/fip_sign.sv, the same player's entry: fip with its cell's product written as an unsigned core and sign rows) with 3 lines replaced by 21: one line of the weight-correction unit gemm_wcorr by eight, and two lines of the row module gemm_rowf, a declaration and the accumulator add, by thirteen. The other 676 lines are fip_sign's. The declaration is fip_sign's, byte for byte. The file keeps fip's header comment, which still names fip and its generator. The player made the two changes one after the other: the weight-correction products first, then the adder.

Why it scores as it does

The machine is fip_sign's, so the cycles are too: 4,573,825 per job, measured on the scored job and equal to the declared formula. What changes are the row, of which there are 128, and the weight-correction unit, of which there is one:

The adder costs area: each row grows by 5.53 µm² (+4.6 %), 707 µm² over the 128 rows. The weight-correction products save 173 µm² (−1.7 %) in their unit. The whole design goes from 3.3411 to 3.3420 mm² (+0.03 %): the on-chip memory, 0.753 mm², and every other module's text are fip_sign's, but synthesized again in this check they come out 359 µm² larger in all, 304 of it in the rows' output stage gemm_scale.

The period is the row module's own register-to-register path: 1,809 ps in fip_sign, 1,735 ps with the carry-select add (the add is the row's only change), 4.1 % less. The row is still the slowest module; the next is gemm_cellf at 1,699 ps (the cell, whose text is fip_sign's).

Score: 3.3420 mm² × 4,573,825 cycles × 1,735 ps = 26.52 mm²·ms per job, 0.657 × par and 0.959 × fip_sign.

Why the sum and the products are exact

The adder. fip_sign writes wd = add_a + add_b at 48 bits, with add_a the accumulator word read from memory (0 when a sum starts) and add_b the sign-extended inner product. The new add takes the operands byte by byte: the low slice adds bytes 0 into nine bits, its ninth bit the carry into byte 1; each further slice g computes both ag + bg and ag + bg + 1 in nine bits and takes the one its incoming carry cg selects, the low eight bits as byte g of the sum and the ninth as the carry into byte g + 1. So byte g of the sum is (ag + bg + cg) mod 28 and its carry out is the quotient, which is schoolbook addition in base 28; by induction over the six slices the 48 bits are (add_a + add_b) mod 248, fip_sign's sum for every input. The carry out of bit 47, which fip_sign's 48-bit add drops, is dropped too (it is computed and not read).

The weight-correction products, as fip_sign's cell product. Read the 16 bits of the two weights as unsigned numbers ua and ub; in two's complement a = ua − 216·a[15] and b = ub − 216·b[15], so a·b = uaub − 216·a[15]·ub − 216·b[15]·ua + 232·a[15]·b[15]. The core is the first term, exactly (32 bits); {1'b0, w[32*t+16 +: 16], 16'b0} is 216·ub, subtracted when a is negative, the other row is 216·ua, subtracted when b is, and 33'sh100000000 is 232, added when both are. The sum is taken at 33 bits, so prod is a·b modulo 233, its low 32 bits are a·b modulo 232, and p = -prod[31:0] is −a·b modulo 232: bit for bit fip_sign's -($signed(a) * $signed(b)) at 32 bits, for every one of the 232 weight pairs. The check's simulations agree (every result right at the four check points, the gate netlist at LGE = 2, the scored job twice); the arguments are what cover every input.

What did not work

Before the carry-select adder the player tried four other ways to shorten the row's or the cell's path, all set aside (the player's notes): registering the row's memory operands, with two more drain cycles, and registering the row's write value with its control both gave wrong results in the check; computing the sum with both memory blocks' words before choosing between them passed the check's simulations but made the row slower (1,973 ps on the player's machine); and a two-stage cell, tried in seven variants, gave wrong results in every one.

The proof

None: like every entry of this task, it is checked by simulation, and the composed score is not a routed one (the task page says what the check does not show).
