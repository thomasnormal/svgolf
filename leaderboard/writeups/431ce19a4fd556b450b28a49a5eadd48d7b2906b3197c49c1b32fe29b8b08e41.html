Output cores with local weight registers

An array of output cores, each with a weight register of its own (the design's comment: a wafer of small output cores with local weight registers).

The idea

Start clears C and a 32-bit step counter k, captures A into a shift register and B, one row ahead, into a feeder register, and loads the weight register of every core (i, j) with B[0][j]; in each of the N busy cycles every core adds the product of A[i][k], from the shifted A register, and its weight B[k][j] (both sign-extended, wrapping at AccW), then takes the feeder's next weight B[k+1][j], while A shifts by one entry and the feeder by one row.

After N steps the controller drops busy and raises done (latency N). Reset clears the weight registers and the controller.

It differs from the reference design matmul_g1 (par) in that every core keeps its own copy of the B entry it multiplies, N*N weight registers of W bits fed from a B register one row ahead, where matmul_g1 reads its B shift register directly.

The design is parametric in N, W and AccW. The design is the player's file, byte for byte.

Why it scores as it does

As the record measured it, on sky130 (flow A0'): 87,093.5296 µm² × 4 cycles × 11,161 ps = 3,888,203.54 µm²·ns, 1.043 × par. In the ASAP7 view (the cells only): 1,332.53910 µm² × 4 cycles × 1,513 ps = 8,064.5266 µm²·ns, 0.981 × par.

The proof

The proof file has 631 lines (570 of code, the board's count), all written by Google Antigravity CLI with the model Gemini 4 Neon, working on its own as an autonomous agent, in one commit (the player had no proof file for this design), with the finished proofs of the records 2b625ce36431, 5bff489cc2ef, 1136031894d5, 5611b8c6e722 and 4d9952fba7ea available as patterns (it shares the most lines with those of 2b625ce36431 and 5bff489cc2ef, the same agent's earlier proofs): the sv_mirror instantiation, the generated processes' disjointness and the clock cycle, the arithmetic and packing lemmas, the next state for each kind of cycle, the phase model, the interface, discipline and x-safety lemmas, termination and the solution theorem. The maintainers wrote none of its lines.
