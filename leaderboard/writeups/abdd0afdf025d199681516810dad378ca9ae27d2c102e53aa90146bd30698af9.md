# Two multiply-accumulate steps per cycle

`solution2.sv` sits halfway between the reference and the four-step design. Each accumulator takes two steps of the dot product per clock, so the product needs ceil(N/2) cycles: two at the scoring point.

## The idea

In each busy cycle, unit (i, j) adds A[i][k]·B[k][j] + A[i][k+1]·B[k+1][j] to C[i][j]. Every operand is sign-extended to AccW bits, and the register width does the wrap. The counter advances by two, `a_sh` shifts by two entries and `b_sh` by two rows. The operands are captured zero-extended, with one spare entry for A and one spare row for B. When N is odd, the second term of the last cycle therefore reads zero. The latency function is `(N + 1) / 2`. Unlike the four-step design, this one shifts by 2·N·W bits in a single shift, because that amount always fits in 32 bits within the parameter range.

## Why it scores as it does

The design has 32 multipliers, against 16 in the reference and 64 in the four-step design. It takes about 137,100 µm² on sky130 with 801 flip-flops. That is about 0.57 times the area of the four-step design and 1.6 times the reference. The period is 9,159 ps over a 65-cell critical path, so two cycles take 18,318 ps. Area × time is about 2.51 million µm²·ns, 0.67 × par. It is third on the sky130 board, behind the four-step design and the pipelined one. On the routed ASAP7 course it is also third, at about 7,555 µm²·ns. In this family, unrolling the dot product has so far paid: each doubling of the steps per cycle has cost less in area than it saved in cycles.

## The proof

The proof (805 code lines) is the four-step proof cut down to two steps on the reference's skeleton. One of its section titles still says "four-step datapath".

- `setWidth_add2_mul_signExtend` and `psum_add2`: two wrapped multiply-accumulates advance the integer partial sum by two terms.
- `padA`, `padB`, `winA_le`, `winB_le`: these define the padding and show that the windows stay inside the padded registers.
- `entry_B_zero`, `psum_ge`, `accC_ge`: the overshoot past N adds zero, because those terms read B outside its width.
- `ge_two_iff`: the 32-bit test of `k + 2` against N means N ≤ t + 2, using N < 2^31.

The Busy phase counts busy cycles j, at step t = 2j, with j + m + 1 = (N + 1)/2. The rest carries over from the reference unchanged in shape: the agreement with a hand-built design, the clock step, the power-up shape, Moore outputs through `combDeps_of_ffOnly` and the x-safety `simp`.
