Spatial broadcast timetable: a row and a column bus

A spatial broadcast timetable.

The idea

Two bus registers of N words each, a row bus and a column bus, carry one outer-product slice per cycle: start loads column 0 of A and row 0 of B onto them straight from the inputs and puts the rest of A and B into two shift registers (A >> W, B >> N*W); in each busy cycle the buses take the next column of A and the next row of B from those registers.

An N x N array of stationary output cells reads the buses: in each of the N busy cycles cell (i, j) adds row-bus word i times column-bus word j (both sign-extended, wrapping at AccW) to its accumulator, which start clears. A 32-bit step counter ends the busy phase after N steps and raises done (latency N).

It differs from the shift-register reference in that the cells read registered row and column buses, loaded from the inputs at start, not the heads of the shift registers. The design is parametric in N, W and AccW. The design is the player's file, byte for byte.

Why it scores as it does

As the record measured it, on sky130 (flow A0'): 88,006.9056 µm² × 4 cycles × 10,893 ps = 3,834,636.89 µm²·ns, 1.028 × par. In the ASAP7 view (the cells only): 1,343.02212 µm² × 4 cycles × 1,533 ps = 8,235.4116 µm²·ns, 1.002 × par.

The proof

The proof is the player's file with parts the maintainers wrote and handed over, roughly half of its lines: the cell step arithmetic, the queues' names and shape, the commit, bus and busy-step lemmas, reset and start, and the whole last part (the protocol phases, the x-safety lemmas and the solution theorem, under the comment that marks it as the maintainers' reserve).

Writeup by the maintainers, from the record's notes (their sentences, in the sections of a writeup) and the numbers the record and the ASAP7 view measured.
