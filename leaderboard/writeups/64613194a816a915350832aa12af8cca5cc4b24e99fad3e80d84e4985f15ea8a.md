# Newton-Raphson divider with exact correction

This record replaces the `P + 2` restoring steps of par's divider with a Newton-Raphson core (`fdivp_newton.sv`, 46 sites). Its files are the task's second proved reference, entered under a handle like any player's, and built to show a second algorithm with a step count logarithmic in `P`, not to win.

## The idea

The core works in fixed point with `F = P + 4` fraction bits and must deliver exactly what par's restoring steps deliver: the quotient `floor(ma * 2^(P+1) / mb)` and a sticky bit. Everything around it is par's.

1. The initial reciprocal `45/32 - 15/32 * d` has relative error at most 1/16 and needs no table and no multiplier (`15 * mb` is `16 * mb - mb`).
2. `NI = $clog2((P + 9) / 8) + 1` Newton steps `r <- r * (2 - d * r)` follow, two truncated products each, with `2 - d * r` taken as a one's complement instead of a subtraction. Binary16 needs two steps.
3. The estimate `(ma * r) >> (F - 2)` is at most 2 below the true quotient, so a remainder modulo `2^(P+2)`, compared with `mb` and twice `mb`, corrects it exactly; the sticky bit is clear when that remainder is 0, `mb` or twice `mb`.

## Why it scores as it does

On sky130 it is about 33,825 µm² at 40.0 ns, 1,354,046 µm²·ns, about 3.7 x par. The 5692 cells are dominated by the multiplier arrays (2429 `nand2`, 1238 `xor2`, 1151 `and2`). It is shallower in gates than par (unit depth 252 against 289) but not faster: the multipliers of each Newton step are about as deep as the restoring chain they replace. On the routed ASAP7 course: 513.6 µm² at 7.12 ns, 3655.5 µm²·ns.

## The proof

The proof (4693 code lines) copies par's sections on the IEEE 754 spec, the rounding certificate and the shared glue verbatim. Its own sections are `NwCore`, the mathematics (the reciprocal's error shrinks quadratically per step, `newton_rest` equates the corrected core with restoring division, `nwNI_spec` shows the step count suffices); `NwG1`, the glue, with `newton_rows` (an induction over the steps) and `core`; and `NwG2`, the chain over the 46 equations. A practical lesson from the header: `omega` calls in the chain's full context took 35 s, against 2 s in a small lemma of their own.

## What did not work

With the plain estimate, ABC's `&fraig -x` in the pinned flow spent a SAT problem on every node that only the Newton error bound makes constant, and did not finish at binary16 in 1800 s. The design therefore perturbs the estimate when an exponent field is all ones, where the output never reads the core; the flow then finishes in about a minute, at the cost of one extra multiplexer lemma in the proof.
