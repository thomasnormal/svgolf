Ripple-carry adder with NAND-majority low carries

A parametric N-bit ripple-carry adder that replaces the XOR-based carry formula on bits 0 and 1 with a direct NAND-majority expression. On sky130 A0' at N = 16 it improves on the previous best record while staying within 2.5 µm² of the ripple-carry area floor.

The idea

In the reference ripple-carry adder (add_n), every bit computes p[i] = a[i] ^ b[i] and c[i+1] = g[i] | (p[i] & c[i]). Under the sky130 A0' mapping, xor2_1 is substantially slower than or2_1 or nand2_1, so the critical path starts at b[0] through xor2_1 before entering the 15-bit nand2_1 carry chain.

Using c[i+1] = ~(~(a[i] & b[i]) & ~((a[i] & c[i]) | (b[i] & c[i]))) on bits 0 and 1 removes the initial xor2_1 from the carry launch: carries c[1] and c[2] emerge via fast or2_1/nand2_1 gates before p[2] = a[2] ^ b[2] is even needed, while bits i >= 2 keep the compact g[i] | (p[i] & c[i]) form.

Why it scores as it does

At N = 16 on sky130 A0', the netlist has 82 cells (50 nand2_1, 28 xor2_1, 2 xnor2_1, 2 or2_1) totaling 462.94 µm² (+2.50 µm² over par's 460.44 µm²) and 2449 ps delay (-162 ps vs par's 2611 ps), giving 1133.75 µm²·ns (0.943 x par, 0.964 x the previous best 9e0b4286ba44).

The proof

The Lean proof follows tasks/add/reference/add_n.lean directly: sv_structure with a simple ripple rank closes CombDesign and Acyclic, sv_interface checks the ports, sv_equations extracts the split low/high carry equations, and a single 8-case lemma maj_eq_carry bridges bits 0 and 1 into CombLib.AddView and add_spec.

What did not work

Applying the majority form to 3 or more low bits added area faster than it reduced delay (bits 2 and above already wait for the incoming carry rather than a[i] ^ b[i]), and full prefix trees lost on sky130 A0' area x time despite lower delay because of higher cell area (though they win on ASAP7).
