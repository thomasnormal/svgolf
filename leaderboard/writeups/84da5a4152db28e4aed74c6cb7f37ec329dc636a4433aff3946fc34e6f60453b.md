# Registered mask round-robin

The par design for the arbiter: a textbook mask round-robin whose grant sits in a flip-flop, written as one `always_comb` and one `always_ff` block and proved for every `N ≥ 1` with `N·N < 2^31`.

## The idea

The state is two `N`-bit registers: the grant `gnt` and a thermometer `mask` marking the clients above the last winner. Each cycle the comb block forms `mreq = req & mask`. If any masked requester remains, the lowest of them wins; otherwise the lowest requester overall wins, which is the wrap-around. Both picks use the lowest-set-bit trick `x & (~x + 1)`. On the edge `gnt` takes the pick and, when something was granted, the mask becomes `~((pick << 1) - 1)`, every client strictly above the winner. Reset clears the grant and fills the mask with ones.

No comb block drives an output, so the design is Moore by construction, as the task requires. Fairness comes from the mask: a client that asks and loses can only be passed over by clients between the last winner and itself, so it is served within `N` cycles.

## Why it scores as it does

At `N = 8` on sky130 it is about 769 µm² in 103 cells, 16 of them flip-flops (eight for the grant, eight for the mask), with a 2,826 ps period: about 2,175 µm²·ns, which is par. Between the flops sit the masked AND, a zero test, two lowest-bit isolations, a select and the mask update; the critical path is 15 cells, unit depth 17. On the routed ASAP7 course it is about 12.4 µm² at 470 ps, 5.8 µm²·ns.

## The proof

The 487-line proof takes the reactive route, `ReactTask.satisfiedAt_of_rank`, which turns the temporal statement into facts about one clock cycle. `rr_sem_eq` shows the loaded design is a hand-built model; one helper, `inst_ne_fill`, lowers `mreq != '0`, where the instantiation simp set stops. The library's closed-form clock step covers flop-only designs, so the proof settles its own comb block (`settle_rr`: one pass computes it, a second changes nothing), then builds the cycle in closed form (`rr_cycle`, `rrNext_reset`, `rrNext_run`).

The invariant `ArbInv` says every signal but the clock is known and the mask is a thermometer. A client's rank is its cyclic distance from the mask's edge, at most `N - 1`. Safety is `pick_grants`; progress is the library's `RoundRobin.step`, under which a requester that loses moves strictly closer to the edge. The structural obligations (Moore output through `mooreOut_of_combTargets`, termination, x-safety, every power-up state through `powerUp_cons`) are short `simp` proofs.

## What did not work

Two open-source arbiters were tried first and neither loads yet: OpenTitan's tree arbiter declares its nodes inside generate blocks, and the verilog-axis arbiter instantiates a priority encoder over unpacked arrays. Rather than rewrite them for the tool, the task took this design as its reference.
