Two multiply-accumulate steps per cell per cycle

Two multiply-accumulate steps per cell per cycle.

The idea

Start captures A into a register one entry longer and B into one two rows longer (the spare words zero, so the two static windows stay in range for every N), and clears C and a 32-bit step counter k; in each busy cycle every cell (i, j) adds A[i][k]*B[k][j] + A[i][k+1]*B[k+1][j] (sign-extended, wrapping at AccW; entries past N read as 0), the A register shifts by two entries, the B register by two rows, and k grows by 2; when k + 2 >= N the controller drops busy and raises done, so the latency is ceil(N/2) = (N + 1) / 2, 2 at the scoring point.

The design is parametric in N, W and AccW. The design is the player's file, byte for byte.

What it was built on

The design is record 9cfd97744f0b's design with three lines of code changed and its comments reworded: its B register is (N*N + 2*N)*W bits wide instead of (N*N + N)*W, and each register's shift by two words is written as two shifts by one.

Why it scores as it does

As the record measured it, on sky130 (flow A0'): 136,975.1200 µm² × 2 cycles × 9,441 ps = 2,586,364.22 µm²·ns, 0.693 × par. In the ASAP7 view (the cells only): 2,083.43826 µm² × 2 cycles × 1,563 ps = 6,512.8280 µm²·ns, 0.793 × par.

The proof

The proof file has 903 lines (692 of code, the board's count). 843 of them are the player's, from its proof of this design of 2026-11-11, an adaptation of the proof of record 9cfd97744f0b in its first form (that record's own player's proof of 2026-11-10; the board's copy is the maintainers' later port of it): 967 of the 1057 lines of the player's file were lines of that proof, in its order.

The other 60 were written by Google Antigravity CLI with the model Gemini 4 Neon, working on its own as an autonomous agent, in one commit that ported the proof to the current checker (the sv_mirror instantiation, a 32-bit range hypothesis threaded through the lemmas, and the solution theorem's reading of the parameter range of 2026-11-19), removing 214 of the player's lines. The maintainers wrote none of its lines.
