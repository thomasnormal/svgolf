# Level-by-level Karatsuba, 4-bit schoolbook base

This record is a full Karatsuba recursion for the carry-less product, written for every `N` as generate loops over the levels of the recursion (`kara_lv.sv`), with 4 x 4 schoolbook products at the leaves. It is the smallest clmul design on the board as of 2026-11-13, and its proof is written by hand.

## The idea

The operands are zero-padded to `P = 4 * 2^L` bits with `L = $clog2((N + 3) / 4)`. Level `l` holds `3^l` nodes, packed side by side in one row of the 2-D arrays `av`, `bv` (operands) and `rv` (products). Going down, each node hands its three children the low halves, the high halves and their XOR. At the bottom, each node multiplies 4 x 4 bits by schoolbook, each column a small balanced XOR tree. Going up, each node combines its children's products as `L + x^m (M + L + H) + x^2m H`, column by column. Everything is indexed part selects with genvar arithmetic. At `N = 16` that is nine base multipliers and 144 partial-product ANDs before synthesis, against 256 for the schoolbook array.

## Why it scores as it does

Area falls and depth rises. On sky130 it is about 2,733 µm², 20% below par's 3,430 µm², but the recursion puts XORs both before the multipliers (the operand sums of the middle children) and after them (the combination columns): unit depth 8 against par's 6, and 1.81 ns against 1.11 ns. The product is 4946.0 µm²·ns, about 1.29 x par, so the smallest design ranked last on this course. The 368 cells are mostly `xnor2` (160) and `and2` (119). On the routed ASAP7 course: 40.28 µm² at 288 ps, 11.60 µm²·ns.

## The proof

The `sv_comb` tactics refuse the design at the `**` in the localparam `P`, so the proof (2175 code lines) works directly on the semantics, for every `N` in range, in three parts:

1. The design at symbolic `N`: the generate templates are pinned by `rfl` and instantiated at a symbolic node `(l, t)`, with new evaluation lemmas for `>>` and `**` and for 2-D part selects at genvar arithmetic (`kara_instantiate2`).
2. Structure: one driver per bit from pairwise-disjoint window lists (`ws_pairwise`), every window inside one row and node slot (`kM_comb`), and acyclicity from a rank that orders operand rows, the base row, then product rows bottom up (`kM_acyclic`).
3. The settled state: `opd_inv` says what each operand row holds; `base_inv` checks the seven base columns; `prod_inv`, by induction from the base level up, says each product slot holds the carry-less product of its node's operands, one Karatsuba step per level (`karatsuba_full`, `comb_spec`); `cl_trunc` drops the padding in `kara_settled`.

The record's tactic list is just `decide`, `induction`, `omega` and `simp`, with a build time of about 15 s.
