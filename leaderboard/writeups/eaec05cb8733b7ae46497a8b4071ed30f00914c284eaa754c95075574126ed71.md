# Radix-4 SRT divider with carry-save remainder

The top-ranked `fdivp` record replaces par's `P + 2` restoring steps with an unrolled radix-4 SRT core adapted from the open-source CORE-V Wally processor (Apache-2.0 WITH SHL-2.1; the header lists the sources and each change with its reason). Its files are the task's third proved reference, entered under a handle like any player's. Everything outside the core is par's datapath.

## The idea

Restoring division resolves one quotient bit per step with a full subtraction. SRT resolves two bits per step with no carry chain inside a stage:

- `N = (P + 2) / 2 + 1` stages, 7 at binary16, each choosing a digit from -2 to 2;
- the partial remainder stays in carry-save form, so each stage costs one carry-save row;
- the digit comes from comparing an estimate of the remainder's top bits with four constants picked by 3 divisor bits, written as `?:` trees;
- on-the-fly conversion appends each digit to the quotient `U` and to `U - 1`;
- one adder at the end sums the remainder, whose sign picks `U` or `U - 1` and whose zero test gives the sticky bit.

The adaptation unrolls Wally's iterative loop into combinational stages, drops square root, rewrites struct parameters, instances, signed comparisons and an `x` default into forms the checker and the tactics read, and keeps the pad bits of the negative multiples constant zero to save area.

## Why it scores as it does

On sky130 it is about 12,357 µm² at 27.6 ns, 340,950 µm²·ns, about 0.93 x par: the only divider on the board under par as of 2026-11-13. It is 28% larger than par but 27% faster, with unit depth 189 against 289 and a critical path of 173 cells to `q[0]`, in 2012 cells. On the routed ASAP7 course it is 187.2 µm² at 5.68 ns, 1063.0 µm²·ns, ahead of par's 1066.7 by a fraction of a percent.

## The proof

The proof (4951 code lines) copies par's sections on the IEEE 754 spec, the rounding certificate and the shared glue verbatim. Its own section `SrCore` is the mathematics: `srt_tbl` checks the selection table cell by cell in one kernel `decide` (the constants form a thermometer code, and every cell's digit keeps the remainder in range); `srt_est`, `srt_qds`, `srt_csa` and `srt_otf` cover the estimate, containment, the carry-save row and the conversion; `srt_step` and `srt_core` carry the invariant `SrtInv` over the stages; and `srt_rest` shows the corrected quotient and sticky bit equal those of `P + 2` restoring steps. Two glue sections connect the 69 printed equations to that core and to par's model. Ten mutants of the core, among them single selection constants off by one to three, all fail the proof. It is the slowest of the task's three reference proofs to check; the record lists a build time of about 112 s.
