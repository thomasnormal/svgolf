# N² multiply-accumulate units, one step counter

This is the par design for the matmul task, `matmul_g1.sv`: one multiply-accumulate unit per entry of `C`, and a single counter that walks the shared index k. Every other record is measured against it, and its proof is the template most later proofs follow.

## The idea

On `start` the design copies `A` and `B` into the shift registers `a_sh` and `b_sh` and clears `C`. In each of the next N busy cycles, unit (i, j) adds A[i][k]·B[k][j] to its accumulator. Both operands are sign-extended to AccW bits by hand, and the register width does the wrap modulo 2^AccW. After each step `a_sh` shifts by one entry and `b_sh` by one row, so every select is a static window and no run-time index appears. `done` rises after the N-th step and holds, with `C`, until the next start. The reset clears only `busy`, `done` and `k`.

## Why it scores as it does

At the scoring point (N = 4, W = 8, AccW = 32) the design has 16 multipliers and 16 accumulators, 802 flip-flops and about 87,170 µm² on sky130. The critical path is one multiply-add into an accumulator, 60 cells long, for a period of 10,696 ps. Four cycles take 42,784 ps, a score of about 3.73 million µm²·ns, which is par by definition. On the routed ASAP7 course it scores about 8,886 µm²·ns at a 1,606 ps period. The score is area × cycles × period, so a challenger can win on any of the three factors.

## The proof

The proof (653 code lines) holds at every in-range (N, W, AccW) and supplies what the library's phase reduction asks for:

1. `mm_sem_eq`: the loaded design is the hand-built `mmD`, stage by stage through the nested generate loops (`mm_gens`, `pe_inst`).
2. `mm_cycle`: one clock edge, from any state of the register shape and under any schedule, is the evaluated commit `mmNext`, via the library's clock step for flip-flop-only designs.
3. The invariant on k: `pe_step` shows that a busy cycle extends each accumulator's wrapped partial sum by one term (`accC`), and `accC_N` identifies the sum at N with the spec's `product`.
4. `model`: the phases Ready, Busy x m and Fin, with their one-cycle obligations.
5. The three rules: every power-up state has the model's shape (`mm_powerUp`); every process is an `always_ff`, so no output has a combinational path (`mm_moore`); and a `simp` shows there is no x-creating construct (`mm_xsafe`).

Each of the task's six attack designs, the reference with one change, fails this proof at `mm_ctrl`, `mm_decls` or `pe_inst`.
