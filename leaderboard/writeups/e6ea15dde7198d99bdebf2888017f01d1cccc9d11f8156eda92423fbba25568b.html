Static timetable of operand words

A static timetable of operand words.

The idea

Generated continuous assignments lay out, at elaboration, a private window of N words for every output cell (i, j) in two N*N*N*W-bit timetables: word t of the cell's window is A[i][t] in the A timetable and B[t][j] in the B timetable.

Start copies both timetables into two stream registers and clears a 32-bit step counter k; in each of the N busy cycles the controller shifts both whole streams right by one word, and every cell of an N x N array, each with its own accumulator (cleared on reset or start), adds the product of the first words of its two windows (both sign-extended, wrapping at AccW). After N steps the controller drops busy and raises done (latency N). Nothing selects an operand at run time.

It differs from the shift-register reference in that every cell has its own copy of its row of A and its column of B, N*N*N words per operand where the reference shifts N*N.

The design is parametric in N, W and AccW. The design is the player's file, byte for byte.

Why it scores as it does

As the record measured it, on sky130 (flow A0'): 125,978.3232 µm² × 4 cycles × 10,470 ps = 5,275,972.18 µm²·ns, 1.415 × par. In the ASAP7 view (the cells only): 1,925.09946 µm² × 4 cycles × 1,459 ps = 11,234.8804 µm²·ns, 1.367 × par.

The proof

The proof file has 806 lines (739 of code, the board's count). Twelve of them are the player's: the header with the latency comment, the imports and the sv_mirror declaration.

The other 794 were written by Google Antigravity CLI with the model Gemini 4 Neon, working on its own as an autonomous agent, with the finished proofs of the records f14f8e008cc0 and ddc2d1b271db available as patterns: the generated processes' disjointness and the clock cycle, the timetable packing and the accumulator arithmetic, the next state for each kind of cycle, the phase model, the interface, discipline and x-safety lemmas, termination and the solution theorem. The maintainers wrote none of its lines.
