# Wallace tree as a queue of rows

A Wallace-tree multiplier for every `N`, written as a tape: one 2-D array `T` of `3N-1` rows of `2N` bits used as a queue, where each carry-save adder takes the three oldest live rows and appends a sum row and a carry row.

## The idea

Rows `0` to `N-1` are the partial products (row `j` is `a` shifted by `j` and gated by `b[j]`, a gather read of a zero-padded copy of `a`), and row `N` is a zero dummy, so `N+1` rows are live. Adder `g`, for `g` from 0 to `N-2`, reads rows `3g`, `3g+1`, `3g+2` and writes their XOR at row `N+1+2g` and their shifted majority at `N+2+2g`. Each adder consumes three rows and adds two, so after `N-1` adders exactly two rows are left, at `3N-3` and `3N-2`, for a ripple-carry adder. This is Wallace's grouping by threes, the live rows shrinking by about 2/3 per level.

The tape position replaces the stage index: every index is affine in `N` and the genvars, with no localparam, division or conditional generate, so the `sv_comb` tactics read the design as written.

## Why it scores as it does

On sky130 it is about 8,966 µm² in 1,502 cells at 5,982 ps: about 53,635 µm²·ns, 0.59 × par. The delay matches the other trees, since the ripple final adder sets the depth (56 cells to `p[30]`, unit depth 62). The area is the cost: grouping whole rows spends a half adder at both edges of every row, where a column-wise Dadda schedule spends almost none. On ASAP7: about 133.9 µm² at 1,264 ps, 169.3 µm²·ns.

## The proof

At 264 lines it is the shortest of the tree proofs. The arithmetic, `TapeArith`, has `csa_row` (a row of 3:2 counters keeps the column-weighted sum up to the dropped top carry), `lane_gated` (a partial-product row's value) and `tape_mul`. Its invariant is the live window: the sum of rows `[3g, N+1+2g)` (`csum V (3g) (N+1-g)`) is `a * b` modulo `2^(2N)` for every `g ≤ N-1`. The proof is by induction on `g`: adder `g` swaps its three rows for the two it appends, which `csa_row` shows have the same sum. At `g = N-1` the window is the last two rows, added by the library's `ripple_row`.

`sv_structure` takes the key `tapeKey` (stage, adder), sound because an adder reads only rows that earlier adders wrote. `sv_interface`, eleven `sv_bridge` lines and `sv_cover`, which recovers adder `(r - N - 1) / 2` from a row read, finish it.

## What did not work

Three variants of the tape did not beat it: the dummy row first, the partial products in reverse order, and the carry row appended before the sum row.
