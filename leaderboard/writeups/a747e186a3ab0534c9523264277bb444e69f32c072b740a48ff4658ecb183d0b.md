# Broadcast weight-stationary array, enabled PEs

`ws_broadcast_en.sv` is the same broadcast weight-stationary array as its sibling record. B is held in the processing elements (PEs) and A is fed through per-row delay lines, whose heads are broadcast across each array row. Partial sums move down the columns, and from cycle N on the finished output rows are shifted into `C`. The latency is 2N, eight cycles at N = 4. Two details differ.

## The idea

First, every PE register is written only while `busy` is 1, so the array stands still between transactions. Second, the operands are sign-extended by a full AccW bits, the form the reference uses, instead of through a localparam that computes max(AccW, W + 1). The control, the delay lines and the collection rows are the same source text.

## Why it scores as it does

The enable buys nothing under this score and costs a little. Both records have 1,266 flip-flops, but this one takes about 90,370 µm² on sky130 against 86,300. Its period is 10,622 ps, over a 57-cell critical path, against 9,802 ps. Eight cycles give about 7.68 million µm²·ns, 2.06 × par on sky130 and 2.0 × par on ASAP7. The files do not separate how much of the difference comes from the enable multiplexers and how much from the wider sign extension.

## The proof

The proof (1,435 code lines) is the free-running record's proof with a handful of lemmas swapped. The pair is therefore a clean comparison of what an enable costs on the proof side.

- Without the localparam, the parameter environment needs no case split, and the `xv`, `declTy` and `tyW` lemmas are gone.
- With the enable, an idle cycle writes nothing. `queues_idle` and `psQ_off` show that the delay-line, partial-sum and collection queues are empty when `busy` is 0. `next_idle` then states that an idle cycle changes no register at all. The free-running proof can only say that the partial sums move to some new value.

The core is shared. `BusyAt` describes the state t cycles into a transaction through the window predicates `DlOk`, `PsOk` and `COk`, and `next_busy` advances it. `eq_ofBitVec_of_win` reads a vector off its windows, and `c_final` gives `C` = A·B after 2N cycles. The phase model has Ready, Busy x m (t busy cycles in, with t + m + 1 = 2N) and Fin. The statement's three rules close the proof: the power-up shape, Moore outputs from a design of `always_ff` processes only, and x-safety.
