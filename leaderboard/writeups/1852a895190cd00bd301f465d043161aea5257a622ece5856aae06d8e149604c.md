# Boyar-Peralta depth-16 circuit, 125 gates

This record is the published depth-16 S-box circuit of Boyar and Peralta, translated gate for gate into `sbox_bp_d16.sv`: 125 continuous assigns over `^` and `&` (34 AND, 87 XOR, 4 XNOR), 117 internal one-bit nets, no generate loop.

## The idea

The netlist has four visible stages: 27 XORs that form linear combinations of the input bits, a middle section that mixes ANDs and XORs, a layer of 18 ANDs that multiplies middle results by signals of the first layer, and 35 XORs that fold everything into the eight outputs. The four XNORs, written `a ^ b ^ 1'b1`, sit on `y[0]`, `y[1]`, `y[5]` and `y[6]`: exactly the set bits of the affine constant `8'h63`.

Against the 113-gate circuit by the same authors, this one spends 12 more gates to cut the published depth from 27 to 16 levels. On this course that trade pays.

## Why it scores as it does

On sky130 it measures about 952 µm² at 3.67 ns, 3494.4 µm²·ns, about 0.44 x par (the 256-entry table). After synthesis it is 131 cells, mostly `xnor2` (56), `and2` (20) and `nand2` (19), with a critical path of 18 cells to `y[1]`. The 113-gate circuit is about 7% smaller (892 µm²) but takes 4.30 ns, so the smaller, deeper circuit loses on the product. On the routed ASAP7 course it is 14.2 µm² at 642 ps, 9.15 µm²·ns.

## The proof

At 125 assigns the netlist is past the budget of the one-line `sv_exhaustive` route, so the proof (209 code lines, generated from the netlist) follows the route of the 113-gate reference:

1. `bp_struct`: `sv_structure` with each gate's depth as the rank (`bpRank`).
2. `bp_interface`: `sv_interface`.
3. `solution`: `sv_equations` hands over the 125 gate equations; a small custom tactic, `gok_chain`, threads them in order into an inductive predicate `GOK` (every gate reads earlier nets and meets its equation).
4. The arithmetic: the netlist as a list of `Gate` records, run by `runN` over one packed 133-bit natural. `bp_correct` checks all 256 inputs against the algebraic `sbox` with `decide +kernel`, and `bp_vals`, an induction over the gate list, shows any state meeting the equations holds the program's values.

## What did not work

Two limits moved from the 113-gate proof: plain `decide` on `bpProg.length = 125` hits the maximum recursion depth (at 113 it fit), so it uses `decide +kernel`; and `solution` timed out at a heartbeat limit of 300,000 and passed at 400,000.
