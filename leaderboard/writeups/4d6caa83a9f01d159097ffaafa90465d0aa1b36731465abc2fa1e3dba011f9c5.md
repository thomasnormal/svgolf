# Static timetable, streamed outer products

`timetable.sv` replaces control logic with a schedule fixed at elaboration. Its only control state is one time counter `t`, and every enable compares `t` with a constant. There is no busy flag, no valid bit and no condition on data. The layout follows the public description of a tensor streaming processor: memory, matrix and vector slices that talk only through stream registers loaded on every clock.

## The idea

- `start` sets `t` to 0, and it counts up to the idle value N + 4, where reset also puts it.
- For the first N cycles the memory slices shift A by one entry and B by one row, so the operand streams carry column t of A and row t of B.
- The matrix slice multiplies all N² lane pairs on every clock, with operands sign-extended only to 2W bits: the exact W × W product.
- The vector slice loads the product stream at t = 2 and adds it up to t = N + 1, in AccW bits.
- A result stream copies the accumulators, and C is written from it at t = N + 3, together with `done`.

The latency is N + 4, which is 8 at the scoring point. Each cycle holds a narrow multiply or an add, never both in series.

## Why it scores as it does

On sky130 the design is 122,543 µm² with 2,145 flip-flops, against 802 in the reference, because every stream and the 2W-bit product stream are registered. The period falls to 5,952 ps, 56% of the reference's 10,696 ps. The extra stages double the latency, though, so the time is 47,616 ps. The score is about 5.83 million µm²·ns: 1.565 × par on sky130 and 1.948 × par on ASAP7.

## The proof

The proof has 1,316 code lines and builds in about 11 s. After agreement (`mm_sem_eq`) and the clock step (`mm_profile`, `mm_cycle`, `commit_MS`), the timetable shape pays off. `c_lt`, `c_le`, `c_gt` and `c_eq` turn each 32-bit comparison of `t` with a constant into a decision on natural numbers.

The invariant `BusyAt t` is a list of facts, each guarded by a range of t: the memories are shifted t times, the streams hold column and row t − 1, the product stream holds `prodV` of step t − 2, and the accumulators hold `accC (t − 2)`. `signExtend_ofInt_prod` shows that a W-bit by W-bit product fits in 2W bits. Entries past the matrix read as zero (`entry_ge`), so `psum_ge` and `accC_ge` let the accumulators keep the name `accC (t − 2)` after the last add. `next_busy`, `next_fin`, `next_rest`, `next_reset` and `next_start` cover the phases of `model`.
