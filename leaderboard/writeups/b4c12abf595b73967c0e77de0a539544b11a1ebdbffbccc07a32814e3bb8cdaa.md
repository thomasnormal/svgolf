# Restoring divider, word-level generate form

This is par for `fdivp`, the simplest algorithm: IEEE 754 division with round to nearest even and full subnormals, generic in the exponent width `W` and precision `P`, in `fdivp.sv`. It is a word-level netlist of 34 sites (D3 in the task notes), proved for every format and scored at binary16.

## The idea

The datapath folds the bit-level structure into vector sites and two generate families:

1. classify the operands (NaN, infinity, zero) with reductions over the fields;
2. normalize each significand in `P - 1` one-bit stages, a wide register counting the shifts down;
3. `P + 2` restoring division steps, one quotient bit each, the last remainder giving the sticky bit;
4. the exponent in two's complement, with normal and overflow flags read from its sign bit and two reductions;
5. for a subnormal result, a run-time right shift with the sticky test `(qs << dsh) != qm`;
6. round by adding the increment to the packed `{exponent, fraction}` word, so the carry moves a subnormal up to the least normal, and the largest finite value to infinity, for free.

A detail from the header: `adj` must be its own one-bit signal, because inline in the wider sum its complement would be taken after the extension.

## Why it scores as it does

On sky130 it is about 9,676 µm² at 38.0 ns, 367,515 µm²·ns, which defines par. The 13 serial restoring steps dominate the delay: unit depth 289 and a critical path of 285 cells to `q[12]`, in 1515 cells. The task notes name the linear normalizer and denormalizer as where logarithmic forms would save area. On the routed ASAP7 course: 147.2 µm² at 7.25 ns, 1066.7 µm²·ns, the smallest area there.

## The proof

The proof (4034 code lines) is self-contained apart from the general library:

- `IEEE754Proofs`: facts about the trusted spec, including its five characterization theorems, such as `divide_isUniquelyCharacterized`.
- `CertF` and its helper sections: `certF_bridge` says, for every format, that the rounded quotient of two finite nonzero operands is `c` exactly when a certificate using only `+ - *`, comparisons and powers of two accepts `c`. Any other divider's proof can start here.
- `DpN` to `DpU`: the datapath as a `Nat` model `OutN`, and `OutN_fdivBits`, equating it with the task function.
- `DpG1` to `DpG5`: one glue lemma per equation shape, and `Glue.chain`, from the 34 printed equations to the value of `q`.

The submission is then short: `sv_structure` with a `rippleRank` (plain sites by depth, the `norm` and `div` families by stage), `sv_interface` after unfolding the task, `sv_equations`, `chain` and `OutN_fdivBits`. The task's eight attack designs, each this netlist with one plausible change (ties to odd, flush to zero, a narrow exponent register), all fail at the `chain` application.
