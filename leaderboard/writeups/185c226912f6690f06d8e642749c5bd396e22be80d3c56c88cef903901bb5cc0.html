Padded sparse-6 Kogge-Stone adder for ASAP7

A parametric N-bit sparse parallel-prefix adder using 6-bit blocks, a Kogge-Stone block-carry tree, and late-carry local ripple. It is tailored for ASAP7, where shallow carry depth pays off strongly against area.

The idea

On ASAP7, ripple-carry adders suffer from a 33-level carry chain (563 ps), whereas full bit-level Kogge-Stone trees pay a large area overhead across all N columns. Grouping bits into 6-bit blocks (Q = (N + 5) / 6, with a and b zero-padded to M = 6 * Q bits) shrinks the Kogge-Stone prefix tree to just Q + 1 positions (4 positions and 2 prefix levels at N = 16).

Within each block, gg and pp compute the 6-bit group generate and propagate signals, the Kogge-Stone tree produces every block carry-in bg[L][j], and a local late-carry chain cc[j][t+1] = x[6*j+t] ? cc[j][t] : ap[6*j+t] fans out carries to the sum XORs. Padding ap and bp to M + 1 keeps the padding generate loop non-empty at every N, including multiples of 6.

Why it scores as it does

At N = 16, the scored netlist has 113 cells and unit depth 20 (down from 33 for ripple and 28 for the previous ASAP7 leader 2387ec6053e6). On ASAP7 it measures 9.28746 µm² x 345 ps = 3.2042 µm²·ns (0.817 x the previous best 3.9195). On sky130 A0' it reaches 1911 ps at 629.35 µm² (1202.69 µm²·ns), trading area for delay.

The proof

SparseAddArith uses LeanModels.Sv.Lib.Prefix (pgOp, pgBlock) to prove the Kogge-Stone recurrence ksRec_block on the 6-bit block pairs, then shows every local carry c_all[i] equals the canonical ripple carry of the padded inputs. sv_structure with a lexicographic rank, sv_interface, and sv_equations connect the netlist to CombLib.AddView and add_spec for all 1 <= N < 2^20.
