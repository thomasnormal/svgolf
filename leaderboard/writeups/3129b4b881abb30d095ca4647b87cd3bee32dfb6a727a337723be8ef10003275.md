# Array multiplier, proved by hand

This record is the task's par design, the `N × N` array multiplier `mul_n`, with a second proof. The design file is a byte-for-byte copy of par; the par proof uses the `sv_comb` tactics and is 70 lines, while this one is 1,385 lines and uses none of them. It is the baseline that shows what the tactics save.

## The idea

Partial products are `pp[i][j] = a[j] & b[i]`. Row 0 is `pp[0]`; each later row adds the upper `N` bits of the row before to `pp[i]` with an `N`-bit ripple-carry adder of full adders. Bit `i < N` of the product is bit 0 of row `i`, and the top half is the upper bits of the last row.

## Why it scores as it does

The netlist is par's: about 8,290 µm² on sky130 in 1,408 cells, 10,907 ps, 90,424 µm²·ns. The carries ripple through every row, so the critical path is 87 cells (unit depth 88), far deeper than the tree designs. On ASAP7: about 123.9 µm² at 2,029 ps, 251.5 µm²·ns.

## The proof

`N`, the inputs and every index stay symbolic; nothing is unrolled or bit-blasted, and the tactics used are `simp`, `omega`, `grind`, `decide` and induction.

1. Arithmetic over `Nat`. The row sums are `R 0 = x·y₀` and `R (i+1) = R i / 2 + x·y_{i+1}`. The invariant `R_inv`, `x * (y mod 2^(i+1)) = 2^i * R i + L` with `L < 2^i`, gives `testBit_mul_row` (bit `k` of the product is bit 0 of row `k`) and `testBit_mul_top` for the upper half.
2. The design at symbolic `N`: the three generate loops expand to a flat list of one-bit cells, `mulProcs N` (`mul_pp_expand`, `mul_row_expand`, `mul_out_expand`, then `mul_sem_processes`), and the one-driver guard passes (`mul_driversOk`).
3. Profile and acyclicity: every process is a slice assign with disjoint write windows (`mul_disjoint`), and a row-major rank `rkWin` is checked reader by reader (`mul_reader`) with `omega`.
4. The settled state (`mul_settled`): by induction on the row, then on the bit within it, row `i` of `s` holds `R i`. The carries are core's `BitVec.carry` (`carry_gate` matches the netlist's AND/OR carry to `Bool.atLeastTwo`) and the sums `BitVec.getLsbD_add`. `CombTask.satisfiedAt_of_comb` turns this into the statement.

In the tactic proof, `sv_structure`, `sv_equations`, `sv_bridge` and `sv_cover` do parts 2 and 3 and the bridging in part 4, leaving essentially the arithmetic. That is why it is about a twentieth of the length; the by-hand file still elaborates faster.
