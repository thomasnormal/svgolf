# X9: operator monotonicity, and how often real blocks decide on x

**Status label:** measurement. Part A is 66 kernel-checked facts about `LeanModels/Sv/Basic.lean` (`decide +kernel`, axioms
pinned; no definition is changed and nothing imports the file). Part B is a pyslang census. Agent M, WS4, 2026-11-02.
Branch `arch-measure`, base `integ1102` at `d5fbdbc4`.

**Question (ARCH-NOTE §7 X9, §4.3 R-X, §9).** Is every pinned 4-state operator table monotone in the information order,
and which are not? How often do real blocks make a procedural decision on a value that could be x? The stop rule: if
non-monotone decisions are common in real blocks, make ⊨² concretization the X-robustness route instead of R-X's
"no decision on x" census.

## A. Monotonicity ([`X9Mono.lean`](X9Mono.lean), MEASURED)

- Cost under load: 102 s wall, 32 s user for the whole file. The checks are kernel `decide` runs, which heartbeats do not
  count.

**Order.** `x` is below every value, and every value is below itself. The variant `XZ` also puts `z` below `0` and `1`;
it gives the same verdict for every operator. Vectors are compared bitwise. A `Bool`-valued decision is monotone iff no
refinement of its input flips it. With `z` incomparable to `x`, `c ? z : z` and `z << x` are reported non-monotone for
that reason alone, so `x ⊑ z` is the right order for R-X (JUDGED; both computed).

**Coverage.** Exhaustive at widths 1 and 2 for every binary operator, and 1 to 3 for unary ones. Nothing is claimed at
other widths.

| verdict | operators (`LVec.*` unless stated) |
|---|---|
| **monotone** | `Logic.and/or/xor/not/merge`; `and`, `or`, `xor`, `not`, `add`, `sub`, `mul`, `div`, `rem`, `sdiv`, `srem`, `shl`, `shr`, `ashr` (signed and unsigned), `pow` (both signednesses), `pos`, `neg`, `merge`, `concat`, `resizeSigned`, `ternary` (`?:`, all three operands); `lt`, `le`, `gt`, `ge`, `slt`, `sle`, `sgt`, `sge`, `eqLogical` (`==`), `neLogical`, `land`, `lor`, `lnot`, `truthy`, `redOr`, `redAnd`, `redXor` |
| **not monotone** (each with a kernel-checked counterexample or `= false` theorem) | `eqCase`/`neCase` (`===`, `!==`; `x === x` is 1, `0 === x` is 0); `eqWildcard`/`neWildcard` (`==?`, `!=?`: an x in the RIGHT operand is a wildcard; `1 ==? x` is 1, `1 ==? 0` is 0); `condTrue` (the `if` decision: `if (x)` takes else, `if (1)` takes then); `matchesCase false/true` (casez/casex item matching); `isKnownZero`, `allKnown` (knownness tests, the `$isunknown` family); `toTwoState` (the 2-state storage boundary, x ↦ 0) |

The expected set (`===`, case matching, `$isunknown`) is confirmed. Three more are measured:
- `==?`/`!=?`, non-monotone in the right operand;
- `if` truthiness itself, as a decision;
- the 2-state boundary `toTwoState`.

The lowering's `Expr2.ifGuard c = ((|c) === 1'b1)` (`Comb2Proc.lean:298`, X3) is built from `===`. It is exactly the
`condTrue` decision written as an expression.

## B. Decisions on a value that could be x ([`x9_census.py`](x9_census.py), MEASURED)

**Reproduce:** `python3.12 -I x9_census.py --rb0 RB0_SRC --repo . --x8src X8_SRC --out x9.json`. Run
`python3.12 -I x9_census.py --self-test` first: a fixture with a reset register, an unreset child register, an
out-of-range select and a pure-input decision. Data: [`x9.json`](x9.json).

**Population.** The 85 RB0 blocks as written (their `corpus.json` files and top) and the 5 X8 core design points. pyslang
compiles each; 7 RB0 blocks compile with slang errors and are marked.

**Method.** A decision site is an `if` condition, or a `case` subject plus its items, in any procedural block. `?:` is not
a decision (A: `ternary` is monotone). The analysis follows the condition's dependency cone backwards:
- through combinational edges (X8's `arcs.Module`, bit-precise, parameters folded);
- through register next-state edges (edge blocks executed the same way);
- through children (exact arcs plus each child's x-capable outputs).

It looks for an x **source** in that cone:
- **unreset-reg**: a register not assigned a constant in its block's leading `if` branch, i.e. no reset or clear;
- **x-literal**: an `'x`/`'z` or x/z digits outside case-item patterns;
- **out-of-range**: a dynamic select whose index type exceeds the range;
- **div**: `/` or `%`;
- **undriven**: read but never written;
- **child-x**: a child output that is x-capable.

Two assumptions: inputs are known (a task's environment rely), and a constant-assigned register is reset. The census
over-approximates, so a site counted "never x" is never x under those two assumptions.

| population | decision sites | could be x | sources (a site can have several) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ibex_core, default / maxperf | 319 / 354 | **17 / 17 (5 %)** | unreset-reg 14, child-x 3 (no x-literal, out-of-range or div anywhere in ibex: every source is an unreset register) |
| cv32e40p_core, default / pulp | 370 / 526 | **1 / 1 (0.3 % / 0.2 %)** | unreset-reg 1 |
| cva6 (cv32a6_imac_sv32 + WT) | 794 | **125 (16 %)** | out-of-range 105, child-x 31 |
| RB0, 85 blocks | 1427 | 515 (36 %) | unreset-reg 492, x-literal 445, child-x 333, undriven 17, out-of-range 6 |
| RB0 without the two generated or don't-care-coded cores (`vexriscv_min_core`, `picorv32_core`) | 930 | **69 (7 %)** | unreset-reg 46 (46 of the 69 sites have no other source), undriven 17 (both in blocks with slang errors), out-of-range 6 |

Of the 61 RB0 blocks with any decision site, 11 have a site that could be x. The heavy cases are:
- `vexriscv_min_core` (SpinalHDL output: 333 of 374 sites, through `'bx` defaults and unreset registers);
- `picorv32_core` (113 of 123: `'bx` don't-cares on unreset registers).

Every possibly-x site in handwritten lowRISC and OpenHW RTL is one of two things. Either it is an unreset register, as in
ibex's compressed decoder: `cm_rlist_q` is reset only when `ResetAll`, and its comment says "assigned before first usage".
Or it is cva6's out-of-range dynamic selects: arrays whose size is not a power of two, indexed by a full-width index.
Both are temporal or value invariants that the static census cannot see, so they are upper bounds.

## Verdict

1. **R-X's operator half is cheap.** Every arithmetic, logical, shift, comparison, reduction, conditional and
   concatenation table is monotone at the checked widths. The non-monotone ones are exactly the knownness-observing
   operators (`===`, `==?` on its right operand, case/casez/casex matching, `$isunknown`-style tests), the `if`
   decision, and the 2-state boundary. A general-width proof (ARCH-NOTE's `fourState_abstracts`) is an induction over
   these definitions; the kernel facts here fix the list it must exclude.
2. **Decisions on x are rare in handwritten cores** (cv32e40p 0.3 %, ibex 5 %, all of them unreset registers). They are
   **common only where the code says so**: generated or don't-care-coded designs with `'bx` (vexriscv 89 %, picorv32
   92 %), and cva6's out-of-range selects (16 %).
   - By the stop rule, R-X with a decision census suits handwritten RTL. There each flagged site becomes a named
     obligation, e.g. a write-before-read invariant for an unreset register or an index-range invariant.
   - Designs that use `'x` as a don't-care need ⊨² concretization. A statement "the same answer under every 2-state
     concretization" cannot hold there by design, because the x is a deliberate synthesis freedom.
3. **The census must read decisions at the settled store or closing activation** (X3, item 3). The counts above are
   static cones, not transient activations.
